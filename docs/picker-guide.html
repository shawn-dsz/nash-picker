Using OnePick

A guide for the person doing the picking. One device, one queue, whatever channel the order came from.

There is nothing to install, no separate app per marketplace, and no login to remember. Open it and start the next order.

Open the app → All docs

01 · The queue

Every order the store owes anyone, oldest first, whether it came from your own website, DoorDash or Uber Eats.

Each row shows the channel, the order reference, the customer's name, how many items are in it and where it is going.

Orders already picked show a green ✓ Picked and the fill rate, so you can see at a glance what is left.

The queue updates itself. New orders appear without pulling to refresh, including while you are looking at it. Tap any order to start.

02 · Walking a run

You get one item at a time. That is deliberate. The screen shows the next thing to put in the tote and nothing else.

The route is already sorted

At the top of every run you will see the walking order:

Route
Produce → Deli → 4 2 moves

The list has been reordered into the way the store is actually walked, not the order the customer added things to their basket. Chilled and frozen come last, so they spend the least time out of temperature.

Follow the order the app gives you. Skipping ahead is allowed, but the route is the shortest sensible walk for that trolley.

Where it is, before what it is

Location sits at the top of the screen, above the product. Until you are standing in front of the shelf, nothing else matters.

If a product has no location recorded you will see No location on file, and you will need to find it the usual way.

The picture is part of the job. Most near-misses are lookalike packaging rather than the wrong aisle, and the picture is the fastest way to catch that before you even scan.

If the system thinks the shelf is empty you will see Shows 0 on hand. That is a warning, not an instruction. Check the shelf anyway, because stock counts drift.

03 · Scanning

Before you can mark most items as picked, you scan them.

The field is already selected, so just scan. The handheld types the number in and confirms it for you. You can also type the digits and press enter.

When it matches, the box turns green, says Barcode verified, and the main button becomes available.

If it is the wrong item

The app tells you what you are holding and what you actually need. Put it back, find the right one, tap Rescan.

This is the whole reason scanning exists. Three colas can sit on the same shelf with almost the same label. The location will never tell them apart, and neither will a quick glance at the end of a long shift.

If you cannot scan it

Loose produce, a torn label, a barcode that will not read. Tap No barcode on the item and carry on.

This is not cheating

Overriding is a normal part of the job and not something to avoid. It is recorded, which is how the store finds out that a whole line has unreadable labels rather than blaming the picker who kept hitting it.

04 · What happened at the shelf

Four things can happen, and there is a button for each.

You got everything

Tap the big button at the bottom. It names what you are confirming - Picked 3, Picked 1kg - so there is no ambiguity about what is going in the tote.

You got some, but not all

Tap Found fewer and set the number you actually have.

For anything sold by weight the button says Enter weight instead. Put the item on the scale and type what it says.

Enter the real number, not the one that was asked for. The customer is charged on what you weighed. A 1kg order of bananas that comes out at 0.94kg is completely normal and the app expects it.

It is not on the shelf

Tap Not on shelf. You do not need to scan first, for the obvious reason that there is nothing in your hand to scan. Nothing else is required and the run carries on.

The customer approved a swap

If the customer chose a replacement when they ordered, it appears as its own button with a picture. Tap it, then scan the replacement to confirm you have the right one.

If more than one alternative was approved they are listed in the customer's order of preference, so take the first one that is on the shelf.

You never choose the substitute yourself. The customer made that decision when they placed the order, with the whole range in front of them.

The customer asked for a refund instead

Some customers say up front that they do not want a replacement. When you see this, no substitute is offered and none should go in the tote.

If the item is not there, tap Not on shelf.

Putting something else in the bag is worse than sending the order short, because they have already told you they did not want it.

05 · Finishing the run

When the last item is done you get the summary. Check it against the tote. This is the last point where a mistake is cheap to fix.

The app saves the run and confirms it. That is picking done.

Delivery is arranged separately and is not started by this app, so the tote still needs to go wherever your store normally stages them.

Tap Back to queue and take the next order.

Worth knowing

You cannot lose a run Everything is saved as you go. If the screen locks, the battery dies or you close the tab, reopen the order and carry on where you left off
Finished orders reopen read-only You can see what was recorded but not change it. If something needs correcting, tell your supervisor rather than re-picking
One picker per order Nothing stops two people opening the same order, so agree who has it before you start
Fill rate is not a score It is what the customer receives against what they ordered. An out-of-stock item is a stock problem, not a picking mistake
