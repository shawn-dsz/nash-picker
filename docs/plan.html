OnePick

Three devices become one queue.

01 — The problem, in FreshMart's words

"Orders come from multiple channels managed on different systems. Our pickers juggle multiple screens and we have no unified view of fulfillment."

FreshMart · 50 stores

A picker on the floor today carries three devices — one for Uber, one for DoorDash, one for the first-party site. Three queues, three interfaces, and no single view of what the store owes anyone.

OnePick is one web app, one queue, one device.

02 — What I found in your API

Nash already models picking end to end. I'm driving your model, not inventing one.

The brief saysNash already calls it
Mark items pickedpicked
Partial quantitiespartially_picked — quantity below requestedQuantity
Out of stocknot_picked
Substitutionssubstituted, driven by subItems[].substitution

pick_and_pack in order.requirements, subItems[] as the pickable unit, items_pick_complete as the completion signal. So the four scenarios aren't a design problem, they're a mapping problem.

03 — Two decisions worth your input

Decision 01 · State
Stateless. Nash holds the state.

No database, no session store. Outcomes are written to pickedItems. Two copies of the truth eventually disagree — and if the tab closes, the run is still recoverable.

Decision 02 · Substitutions
Applied, never chosen.

The customer decided at checkout. The picker confirms in one tap and never searches. Honouring refund matters as much as honouring substitute.

06 — The metric

Order fill rate.
Units delivered over units ordered.

One number that absorbs every unhappy outcome: not picked, partially picked, and a declined substitution all reduce it. FreshMart already reports against it, so it's recognised rather than invented.

Accuracy

Speed
Units / hour
Substitutions

Headline
Fill rate

The cut that matters is by channel. Web filling at 96% while marketplace fills at 89% is invisible when the channels live on separate devices. That comparison is the whole point of unifying them.
